Last Will Drafting & Testamentary Probate Procedure in Sri Lanka

Statutory rules of will execution, proving testaments, Orders Nisi, and Letters of Administration in District Courts under the Civil Procedure Code.

1. Statutory Foundations of a Valid Last Will

Estate planning and the transfer of ancestral or acquired properties across generations in Sri Lanka are primarily governed by the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance No. 7 of 1840. Under Section 4 of this Ordinance, for a Last Will and Testament or Codicil to possess legal validity, it must be executed strictly through one of two statutory modalities:

  • Notarial Attestation (Recommended Standard): The testator signs the instrument in the simultaneous presence of a licensed Notary Public and two attesting witnesses, all present at the same time, who witness the testator's signature and subscribe the document in the presence of each other.
  • Attestation Before Five Witnesses: If executed without a licensed Notary Public, the Will must be executed in the simultaneous presence of at least five competent witnesses who attest the execution together.

Crucial Drafting Precaution: Under Sri Lankan law, any gift, devise, or bequest made in a Last Will to an attesting witness, or to the spouse of an attesting witness, is entirely void. Beneficiaries must never act as witnesses to a Last Will.

2. Mandatory Testamentary Jurisdiction in the District Court

Upon the death of any person leaving property in Sri Lanka, the administration of their estate is governed by Chapter XXXVIII of the Civil Procedure Code No. 2 of 1889. Under current statutory thresholds, if the gross value of the deceased's estate (including real estate, bank deposits, shares, and movable assets) exceeds Rs. 4,000,000, formal testamentary proceedings in the District Court are mandatory by law.

No bank, Land Registry, or financial institution in Sri Lanka can lawfully release funds, transfer registered land folios, or alter property ownership of an estate exceeding the statutory threshold without a judicially issued Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration.

3. Probate of a Last Will (Testate Succession)

Where the deceased left a valid Last Will naming an Executor, the procedure progresses through structured judicial steps:

  • Petition and Affidavit: The Executor files a formal petition in the District Court within whose territorial jurisdiction the deceased resided, accompanied by the original Last Will, certified death certificate, asset inventory, and proxy appointing an Attorney-at-Law.
  • Order Nisi: The court examines the papers, calls the attesting Notary or witnesses if necessary, and enters an Order Nisi declaring the Will proved.
  • Gazette and Press Publication: Notice of the Order Nisi is published in designated national newspapers and the official Government Gazette, and served on all statutory intestate heirs.
  • Order Absolute & Probate: If no caveats or objections are filed challenging testamentary capacity, undue influence, or forgery, the court enters an Order Absolute and issues the formal Certificate of Probate with a court-sealed copy of the Will annexed.

4. Letters of Administration (Intestate Succession)

If the deceased passed away without executing a valid Last Will (died intestate), or if the named executor predeceased the testator or renounced probate, the lawful heirs must apply for Letters of Administration:

  • Preferential Right to Administer: The surviving spouse has the foremost legal entitlement to administer the estate, followed by adult children or next-of-kin.
  • Devolution of Intestate Estate: Estate distribution follows the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance (or Kandyan / Thesawalamai personal laws where applicable). Under general Roman-Dutch principles, the surviving spouse typically inherits one-half share, while the remaining half devolves equally among surviving children.
  • Judicial Conveyance: Once all creditors are settled and court accounts passed, the administrator executes formal Conveyance Deeds transferring specific titled lots to each recognized heir in accordance with the court-approved Final Account.

5. Testamentary Representation for Non-Resident & Diaspora Heirs

Expatriates and members of the Sri Lankan diaspora who inherit real estate or bank deposits in Sri Lanka do not need to physically relocate to Sri Lanka to navigate testamentary probate. A non-resident heir or executor can lawfully execute a Special Power of Attorney (POA) abroad—duly attested before a Sri Lankan Consular Officer—appointing Senior Counsel in Sri Lanka to institute the testamentary petition, attend court call-overs, tender evidence, and execute notarial deeds of conveyance on their behalf.
